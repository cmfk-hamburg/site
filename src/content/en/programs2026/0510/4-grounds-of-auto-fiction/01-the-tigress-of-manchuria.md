"The Tigress" is a novel written by the Russian writer Николай Байков, who was active in Northeast Asia in multiple identities throughout his life, including colonizer, scientist, and exile. From the Russo-Japanese War to the puppet state of Manchukuo, he lived through half a century of exploration and colonization, civilization and barbarism.

The novel is autobiographical and tells the story of a pair of lovers who sacrifice themselves to the tiger in the mountains.

The filming of the work retraces Baikov's route of colonization, scientific research, and exile in the Northeast. Facing geopolitical relics, the unchanging mountains, rivers, and forests, and today's biological activities, these multiple realities are organized together to reconstruct Baikov's story: a story about a self-proclaimed civilized conqueror whose fantasy collapsed and gave birth to a mad impulse to sacrifice nature.

### Sue HUANG

Born in southeast China in 1999, she graduated from the Institute of Space and Image Narrative at the China Academy of Art. Her practice is mainly image-based, attempting to connect fiction, memory, and place.
